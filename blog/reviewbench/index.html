The benchmark

ReviewBench is an open benchmark for AI code review: 219 real pull requests from 187 public repositories in 19 languages. Every reviewer runs on every PR, and its comments are scored against issues that human reviewers confirmed. Recall is the share of those issues a reviewer found. Precision is the share of its comments that were right. The leaderboard ranks by F1, which balances the two.

ReviewBench opens filtered to high severity: 175 of the benchmark's 2,565 confirmed issues. It's the only severity filter in which Copilot's best entry makes the top five. Across all 2,565 issues, klaussy review ranks #3 and Copilot ranks #10. You can check both: all severities and high severity only.

How klaussy review works

Most AI reviewers treat two questions as one:

  • What's wrong with this PR?
  • What does this team want to hear about?

When those share one setting, the only way to cut noise is to find less. You can see it in the table. Every big-name reviewer's comments are right 84–88% of the time, and every one of them misses between 72% and 90% of the issues. Noisy reviewers get uninstalled, so these tools say less, and the bugs they don't mention get merged.

klaussy review splits the two. The review pass looks for everything: bugs, risky changes, style problems, nits. Then your settings decide what gets posted. Turn nits off and it still finds them; it just doesn't post them. Filtering never makes it worse at finding bugs.

Findings land inline on the exact diff line, each with a suggested change and a collapsed explanation. It runs the agent you pick (Claude, GitHub Copilot or OpenAI) on your own API key, across GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket and Azure DevOps.

How it reviews a PR in 55 seconds

The benchmark run used Klaussy's fast mode, which is built for one job: a full review back before you've switched tabs. On ReviewBench it averaged 55 seconds per pull request. The other top entries took between 1 minute 27 seconds and 5 minutes 37 seconds, Copilot Max took 16 minutes, and Codex Max took 9.

Speed matters for review more than it looks. A review that lands in under a minute shows up while the author still has the change in their head. One that lands 16 minutes later arrives after they've moved on, and the fix waits for the next round.

Why it's better

  • It finds more. 58% of confirmed issues, against 28% for Copilot Max and 18.5% for Codex Max.
  • It's fast. 55 seconds per review, the fastest of the 20 entries that report a time. Copilot Max takes 16 minutes and Codex Max takes 9.
  • You choose what it posts. The benchmark run had nits included, with everything posted, to show how much it catches. That's why its precision (80%) sits below Copilot's and Codex's (86–88%). Teams that turn nits off see fewer comments without losing bug coverage.
